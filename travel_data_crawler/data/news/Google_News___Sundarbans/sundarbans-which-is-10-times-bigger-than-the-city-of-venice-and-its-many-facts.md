Sundarbans, the very name casts a spell, and exploring this national park is every wild lover’s dream.
Of late Sundarbans has caught attention of many not because of its obvious fame of being the largest delta in the world, but because of water level issue. Researchers are of the view that the effects of climate change will possibly swallow some of the remote islands in the near future. And if this situation remains the same, it is feared that most of the islands will disappear owing to rising tides.

Also, a part of this mangrove also lies in Bangladesh, and Sundarbans National Park was declared a UNESCO World Heritage in 1987. It is one of the exclusive spots for safaris and excursions in India and West Bengal. And the park has the biggest delta and mangrove forest in the Ganges.
Apart from the Sundarbans being shrouded by the rise of water level issue, some of the facts make this national park even more amusing. Check them out and see if you were aware of these.
102 islands
You will be surprised to learn that the abode of Sundarbans, which is spread across 4000 sqkm, is an archipelago of 102 islands. Out of these 102 islands, 54 are inhabited by people, while the rest of the area is covered by forests. Also, while India owns 40 percent of the total area of Sundarbans, the rest of the area is being owned by Bangladesh.
What’s in the name?
There is an amazing fact related to this. Did you know how this national park got its name Sundarbans? It was actually named after a mangrove tree called Sundari, which translates to beautiful. And no one can deny the fact of this park being nature’s own land and being blessed with bountiful flora and fauna.
Tidal phenomenon of the Sundarbans
Sundarbans experiences high and low tides twice a day. And what happens during these occasions? Well, during high tides, you get to witness the water level rising to a level of around 6 to 10 ft; whereas during low tides, you witness huge mud land area lying flat. If you visit Sundarbans National park and want to explore wildlife there, high tides are the best time for boats to get into the forest and check out the wild animals.
Gosaba
Gosaba is the biggest inhabited island in the Indian part of Sundarbans, which comprises 40 percent of the total area of Sunderbans. After this island, the area for huge dense forest starts. You will be impressed to learn that that this island has its own hospitals, panchayats, government officials, and schools.
Widow Village
There is a village here that goes by the name Widow Village. And the reason behind this is not very heart-warming; it is because of tigers. As per the records, most of the families have lost their family members or husbands to man-eating tigers.
Largest fishery board of India
This area is also popular for its brackish marine fisheries and water fish production. It is in fact one of distinctive and impressive natural resource endowments, and Sundarban Development Board is considered to be the country’s Largest Fishery Board.
