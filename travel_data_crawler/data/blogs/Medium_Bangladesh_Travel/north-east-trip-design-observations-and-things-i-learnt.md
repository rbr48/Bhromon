Open shop, shop open as disguised liquor shops
Almost all the liquor shops in Meghalaya are named ‘Shop Open’ or ‘Open Shop’. This seems interesting as to why is this being done. Is there any government restriction?

Khasi village, Knups
Since it rains too much in Meghalaya, you can not always hold an umbrella and get your hands engaged. Knup is indigeniously designed with bamboo to save your head and back from rain and keep you dry.

They have many many meat shops
I have to many places in south as well as in north. North East was one place where I saw a LOT of non-veg being sold openly. Depicts the spready of non-veg in their diet.

Toilet use and pay are very common. Even then there is no urinal ceramic.
There was a great prevalence of ‘Use and Pay’ all over the state of Meghalaya. There were no free public convinience at all. Sadly even after that the service was not good. They had simply made sections with wooden plank but one had to urinate on wall only :(

So many small cars like Alto, Maruti 800 and modified tyre
Surprisingly, the small cars were most in the user there. Also, most of them had modified tyres. On asking the native, I found that it was done to improve the balancing of car in the high-altitude areas.

Too many ORS sold
Everywhere we went, wo found ORS being sold like Frooti and Appy. This is quite strange as I have been to many places but didn’t see the ORS being sold this much. Is teh dehydration really that big of an issue in Meghalaya?
Guwahati Rickshaw
Again, since it rains too much there, the rickshaw has shed over the driver as well. This was quite sensitive design in the interest of driver. Otherwise other places have shed for only the customer.

Eats a lot of supari, as it grows more here
Supari is produced the most in Meghalaya. Even all the locals who were involved in tourist activities in peak tourist season, they do Supari harvesting in the off-season.

Local time often is caught to be of Bangladesh due to signals.
This was actually a great source of confusion. Whenever we were near the border, our phones where showing the time of Dhaka which is half an hour ahead of Indian Time. This confused us on many occasions. Also in general it felt India needed two time zones.

Pathhar trucks on the border of Dawki and Bangladesh
While you would be visiting Dawki, you will find a long lane (in 5–6 KM) of trucks filled with limestones. Turns out India exports a great share of limestone to Bangladesh. It is an environmental concern after a point though.

Another interesting we observed. While you drive on the road, you see a another state on the other side of the road. So the road in itself is a border. This was a new thing we observed

Pitcher plants in Mawlynnong: the cleanest village
Pitcher plant was something I had only seen in the textbooks till now. Seeing the insect-eating plant in real was a very beautiful experience.

Common Nettle is very common
Well this infectious plant was pretty common in many areas of Meghalaya. It struck me as well by accident and it gets your skin rashes with burning sensations. Locals suggested to have calcium paste along with you as it dilutes the acidic effect.
Press enter or click to view image in full size
Chai served with a small thermos
Very interesting solution to serve hot beverage. Most of the places serve the chai in cups but one restaurant served it in thermos to keep the chai warm. One can pour it whenever they want.
Double charge for wasting food
A bold decision and messaging. One cafe near double decker root bridge had it clearly mentioned that no food should be wasted as it will penalise you to pay double. Well it really had a great impact, my co-travellers who were wasting food everywhere, didn’t waste the food here.
