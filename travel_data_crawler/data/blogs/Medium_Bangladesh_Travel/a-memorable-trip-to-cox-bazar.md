Cox Bazar is Bangladesh’s hidden secret. There is enormous potential for the city to emerge as a nice tourist destination in South Asia. But, we need to be more strategic in building infrastructures and arranging services responsive to the needs of the tourists.
This time, I’ve visited Cox Bazar after 4–5 years. It is great to see many new hotel chains springing up, particularly near Inani beach- a 40–50 minute marine drive from the airport with spectacular views of hungry tides on one side and lush green hills on the other side.
What impressed me most about the service of Bay Watch- a brand spanking international hotel chain spearheaded by Bangladesh Army. They have done a praiseworthy job in providing security near hotel area and also added various restaurants, private beaches, considering the need of the tourists in that particular area.

Here are few observations from the trip as a tourist:
1.This is good to see Cox Bazar is building a new international airport. The airport needs to connect internationally to attract passengers traveling to South Asia. If people want a 2 day transit in Dhaka or want a direct flight from South Asian countries, they will not want to miss out experiencing the stunning sunset at the world’s largest beach.

2. The marine drive road between Cox Bazar and Inani Beach area needs a strong upgrade. There are so many auto rickshaws traveling through this road which becomes risky. For any tourist-local or international, taking a trip from Cox City to Inani is a real hassle. In many countries, I’ve seen the local mayors provide free airport shuttle to the tourists which is a subsidy. Government shouldn’t make it free but should arrange secure and cost-effective shuttle service to make the journey smooth and secure for tourists.
3. What I saw in Europe is that many tourist places are designed friendly for elderly population. Cox Bazar needs to be designed considering the age of diverse populations. For example, the authorities can build a long promenade on the sides of the beach where elderly people can walk uninterruptedly. Many school, college or university students come to Cox Bazar for study trip. The authorities need to give special attention to make the city more welcoming for students and offering special discounts in some places.
4. Cox Bazar needs to be a walkable city. I must say authorities did a good job in building footpaths. Tourists tend to visit places at evening and night in search of food or tea/coffee. I found it a bit clumsy while walking through the footpaths. Also, some street lights were off which made the place dark. The authorities must be proactive in ensuring good street lights and uninterrupted footpaths to make the city walkable and safe. The area adjacent to the beach area is not so big, therefore, it won’t be too difficult to manage the area properly. Also, the city should give a special effort in putting dustbins at the right places and making the footpath clean, so that pedestrians can feel comfort.

5. The city authority should also consider creating cycle lanes where tourists can avail cycles in their hotels for rides in exchange of money. Many tourists might be comfortable in cycling and cyclists can provide rave reviews if they find safe cycle lanes visiting one beach point to another while exploring food. Cycle rallies can also be arranged on a regular interval where international tourists can also participate.
6. We went to Falong Jee- a Burmese restaurant- to taste local cuisine and not everyone knows about such places. One of my cousins took us there and we enjoyed the meal thoroughly. More promotions should be done to promote such local cuisines and this could be a nice way to promote such local foods. Tourists often look for such options, but lack of information can prevent people from tasting local cuisine.

7.The security of the beach should be managed by organizations trained by Bangladesh’s armed forces. The way BayWatch or Shopnil Sindhu or the airport is managed by our armed forces and their association, my hunch is they would do an excellent job in keeping the place safe and clean. The association of armed forces units should create more such service oriented firms where retired armed forces professionals can oversee such services. A tourist needs to feel safe while going to the beach.
8. Many hotel chains are present, yet we could see wastes dumping in areas where there’s no building. We need a mission to make Cox Bazar a clean city by 2030(For example). For this, there should be some dedicated cleaning companies which can look after the cleanliness. Hotel chains in the area can even contribute money to ensure cleanliness in the city.
9.Cox Bazar should host international football, cricket and even BPL matches. It’s such a shame that our rich cricket board is yet to upgrade the stadium in Cox Bazar. Every year, BPL should host cricket matches and also there should be a big football ground to host regular football matches there.
10. I was impressed to see many restaurants in Laboni Beach with great arts and live music which reminded me of cafes in Europe. More such restaurants will follow suit when more tourists will start visiting Cox Bazar.
11. I saw a great nudge in the BayWatch hotel during their check-out time. This is unique and I saw people were complying to it. This type of nudges can work wonders and make our citizens more complying to rules. Such experiments can be replicated in other areas in the service industry.

Cox Bazar should be a role model in ensuring a clean city which can have strong correlation with number of tourist visits and earning revenues. The hotel owners, tourism ministry, sports ministry, security agencies must coordinate their action and build a strong synergy to attract more tourists. This way, our tourism industry can flourish bigtime. This can be measured and improved through data-driven insights by regularly monitoring the tourist numbers and revenues earned in the city. We might see small investments can bring outsized returns if actions can be coordinated.
No doubt there has been modest developments in Cox Bazar in recent years and we hope following reforms can make Cox Bazar more attractive and tourist friendly. I look forward to visiting Cox Bazar every time and see how it’s progressing as an attractive tourist destination.
