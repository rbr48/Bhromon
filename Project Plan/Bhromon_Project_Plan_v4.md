## 2. Vision, Mission & Values

### Vision
A Bangladesh where every traveler — from a university student backpacking in Sajek to a family planning their first Sundarbans trip — has access to verified, current, community-owned intelligence about every corner of this country.

### Mission
Build the definitive, community-owned repository of travel intelligence for Bangladesh — structured, searchable, peer-validated, and accessible without internet connectivity.

### Core Values

**1. Ground-Truth First**
Every feature, every design decision, every moderation policy serves one goal: surfacing what is actually true on the ground, not what generates a booking commission.

**2. Offline as a Right**
Connectivity is a privilege in Bangladesh's most beautiful places. Offline capability is a first-class citizen, not an afterthought. If it doesn't work without signal in the Chittagong Hill Tracts, it doesn't ship.

**3. Radical Transparency**
The Explorer Score formula is public. Moderation decisions are appealable. Partner content is always labeled. No hidden algorithms, no secret boosts, no paid rankings.

**4. Community Owns the Knowledge**
Content created by the community belongs to the community. Explorers are credited, recognized, and rewarded. The platform facilitates — it does not extract.

**5. Safety is Non-Negotiable**
Travel information can have life-safety consequences. A single false safety tip can ruin a trip or endanger a life. Safety flags receive immediate action, not queue management.

**6. Preservation Over Perfection**
The undocumented knowledge of a CHT village elder is more valuable than a polished hotel review. Bhromon dignifies local wisdom and creates permanent, searchable records of knowledge that would otherwise be lost.

## 3. Problem Statement — Deep Analysis

### 3.1 The Information Desert

Bangladesh's domestic travel market is experiencing rapid growth — particularly among youth aged 18–35 — yet the digital information infrastructure is profoundly broken across five dimensions:

#### Dimension 1: Fragmentation
Travel knowledge in Bangladesh exists across 200+ closed Facebook groups, Messenger threads, decade-old blog posts on BlogSpot, and YouTube videos without searchable transcripts. A traveler planning a trip to Sajek must:
- Join 8–12 Facebook groups
- Post questions and wait hours/days for responses
- Cross-reference contradictory information from strangers
- Discover critical facts (washed-out roads, ferry schedule changes) only upon arrival

**Cost to traveler:** 4–8 hours of research that produces unreliable results.

#### Dimension 2: Dangerous Information Asymmetry
The gap between what travelers know before departure and what they encounter on arrival is not merely inconvenient — it can be dangerous:

- **CHT access rules** change seasonally and by political climate; online information is consistently outdated
- **Road conditions** in haor regions and hill tracts are heavily weather-dependent; no real-time update mechanism exists
- **Ferry schedules** in the Sundarbans and Barisal region change without public announcement
- **CNG and rickshaw fares** in tourist areas are systematically inflated for outsiders; no fair-fare reference exists
- **Safety incidents** (robbery corridors, flood zones, unstable bridges) are known locally but never reach travelers

**Cost to traveler:** Financial loss averaging BDT 800–2,500 per trip from information asymmetry; in worst cases, safety incidents.

#### Dimension 3: Commercial Corruption of Trust
Existing digital travel resources in Bangladesh are structurally corrupted by commercial incentives:

- Hotel booking apps surface properties based on commission rate, not quality
- Facebook travel pages monetize through "sponsored" posts indistinguishable from genuine recommendations
- YouTube travel channels receive free stays/meals in exchange for positive coverage, undisclosed
- "Best of" lists in travel blogs are predominantly paid placements

**Result:** Bangladeshi travelers have developed a deep, rational distrust of digital travel advice. Authentic knowledge stays trapped in private conversations.

#### Dimension 4: Ephemeral Knowledge
The most valuable travel intelligence — a working phone number for a reliable boat guide in Tanguar Haor, a warning about a seasonal trail closure, the exact fare for the blue CNG vs the red one — exists in conversations that disappear. No mechanism exists to make this knowledge permanent, searchable, or verifiable.

#### Dimension 5: Connectivity Exclusion
The most spectacular travel destinations in Bangladesh — Sajek Valley, Nilgiri, Tanguar Haor, Sundarban interior, Lawachara — have poor to zero mobile connectivity. Every existing digital travel platform fails completely at these destinations, precisely when travelers need guidance most.

### 3.2 Market Failure Summary

| Problem | Current "Solution" | Why It Fails |
|---|---|---|
| Finding trip info | Facebook groups | Closed, unsearchable, real-time only |
| Verifying tip accuracy | Ask multiple sources | No consensus mechanism, no decay |
| Getting offline access | Screenshot everything | No structure, no map, no sync |
| Fair pricing reference | Ask locals | Locals give outsider prices |
| Safety information | Hope for the best | No real-time crowdsourced system |
| Preserving local knowledge | It disappears | No structured documentation platform |

## 4. Solution Architecture

### 4.1 Platform Philosophy

Bhromon is a **geospatial knowledge graph** — not a booking platform, not a social network, not a review aggregator. The distinction matters:

- A **knowledge graph** structures relationships between places, tips, people, and time
- Every piece of content has a location, an author, a timestamp, a trust score, and a freshness state
- The graph becomes more valuable with every addition because connections between nodes increase exponentially
- No single entity — including Bhromon — can corrupt the graph without the community detecting it

### 4.2 The Core Loop

```
DISCOVER → DOCUMENT → VALIDATE → SHARE → EARN REPUTATION → UNLOCK PRIVILEGES
    ↑                                                              |
    └──────────────────────── Repeat ──────────────────────────────┘
```

Every feature is designed to strengthen one or more steps in this loop.

### 4.3 The Five Pillars

#### Pillar 1 — The Verified Map
Every location pin carries a **Trust Score** computed from:
- Community confirmation count and recency
- Author's Explorer Score
- Flag history (Outdated / Inaccurate / Spam / Safety)
- Time since last confirmed activity

Pins are color-coded: Grey (Unverified) → Mint (Community) → Gold (Verified) → Deep Green (Region Expert) → Amber (Pioneer)

The map is offline-capable: pre-cached OpenStreetMap tiles + downloaded pin data means full navigation at zero signal.

#### Pillar 2 — TripLogs
Structured travel stories built on a block-based editor:

| Block Type | Content | Special Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Text | Rich formatted narrative | Inline translation hints |
| Image | Photo with caption | Auto-compress, EXIF strip, offline cache |
| Video | Short-form travel clip | Auto-compress, video caching |
| Cost Entry | Itemized expense table | Live BDT/USD total |
| Route | Drawn polyline on map | Offline-navigable |
| Safety Note | Flagged warning text | Info / Warning / Danger severity |

TripLogs have a **Completeness Gate**: must include title, ≥1 image/video, ≥1 cost entry, a safety details note, and a drawn route before publish. This structural requirement produces rich, comparable content rather than "nice photos, but where exactly?"

#### Pillar 3 — Pro-Tips Engine
Bite-sized local wisdom with an expiry date.

Every Pro-Tip carries:
- `lastConfirmed` timestamp
- `decayStatus`: Fresh (<30 days) → Aging (30–180 days) → Stale (>180 days)
- `helpfulVotes`, `outdatedVotes`, `flagCount`
- `decayFactor`: 0.0→1.0 weight for feed ranking

When a tip reaches Stale status:
1. A nightly Cloud Scheduler marks it
2. FCM push sent to original author + nearby saved users
3. Decay badge turns red in UI
4. Feed ranking weight drops to near-zero
5. Author or any confirmed user can re-validate with one tap

This creates a **self-cleaning database** — tips either stay accurate or visibly die.

#### Pillar 4 — Offline Vault
One-tap district download. Works at zero signal.

Download includes:
- All LocationPins for the district bounding box
- All TripLogs linked to those pins (full content, all blocks)
- All Pro-Tips for those locations
- Pre-compressed images for all ImageBlocks (max 800px, JPEG 80%)
- OpenStreetMap tile cache (zoom 10–16, ~50 MB/district)

Offline writes queue to an **outbox** in Hive, sync automatically on reconnect, and route to a **three-way merge dialog** on conflict.

#### Pillar 5 — Trust Engine
The Explorer Score system is the immune system of the platform.

```
Score = (helpfulVotes × 2) + (tripsPublished × freshnessDecay)
      + peerEndorsements − (spamFlags × 5) − (safetyFlags × 10)
```

The formula is **publicly displayed** in every user's score breakdown screen. Gaming attempts are visible — any unusual spike in helpful votes from new accounts triggers the flag aggregation pipeline.

Trust tiers gate platform privileges:
- **Unverified** → Post Pro-Tips only
- **Community (≥50)** → Publish full TripLogs
- **Verified (≥200)** → Create Premium Guides
- **Region Expert (≥500 + council nomination)** → Division moderation tools
- **Pioneer (Founding program)** → Permanent, permanent badge, cannot be demoted

## 5. Market Analysis

### 5.1 Market Sizing

**Total Addressable Market (TAM): ~20 million**
Bangladesh's domestic leisure travel market. Post-pandemic recovery has driven consistent growth, with BTRI (Bangladesh Tourism Research Institute) estimating 18–22 million domestic leisure trips annually. Youth-led travel is the fastest-growing segment.

**Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM): ~5 million**
Urban mobile-first travelers aged 18–35 who actively research trips via smartphone. This segment:
- Has 4G LTE access in home cities
- Uses Facebook groups and YouTube for travel research
- Owns a smartphone with ≥2GB storage for offline content
- Makes ≥2 leisure trips per year

**Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM — Year 1): 50,000 MAU**
Achievable through:
- Founding Explorers network effects (30 → 10K within 3 months)
- University ambassador program (15 campuses, ~150 active ambassadors)
- Invite-only FOMO beta launch
- Organic social sharing of TripLog content

**Year 3 Target: 150,000 MAU**
Driven by:
- Proven content quality attracting organic search traffic
- Partnership with Bangladesh Tourism Board
- Word-of-mouth from satisfied travelers
- Premium Guide marketplace attracting top content creators

### 5.2 Market Dynamics

**Tailwinds:**
1. Bangladesh GDP per capita rising → more discretionary travel spend
2. Gen Z and millennial travel culture maturing in urban centers
3. 4G coverage expanding — 60% smartphone penetration
4. Government investment in tourism infrastructure (CHT, Sundarbans corridors)
5. Rise of "workation" culture post-COVID → longer domestic trips

**Headwinds:**
1. Low digital payments adoption → monetization friction
2. Content moderation at scale in Bengali language requires specialized capability
3. Some target demographics still prefer WhatsApp/Facebook over dedicated apps
4. Regulatory uncertainty around CHT content (government sensitivity)

### 5.3 Revenue Opportunity

At 150,000 MAU with 5% premium conversion:

## 8. User Experience Design

### 8.1 Design Principles

**1. Context-aware information hierarchy**
The home screen knows your division, your score, your vault status, and your activity streak. It surfaces the most relevant content for *your* context — not a generic best-sellers list.

**2. Decay is visible, never hidden**
No content that might be outdated should look the same as fresh content. The decay badge system makes information age a first-class UI element, not a hidden metadata field.

**3. Offline confidence, not offline anxiety**
The app should never leave a user uncertain about what's available offline. The vault status bar, district download badges, and offline-ready indicators give constant, unambiguous status.

**4. Trust signals at every touch point**
Every piece of content — every pin, every TripLog, every tip — carries its trust tier, its age, and its confirmation status. Users should never have to wonder "but is this reliable?"

**5. Actions earn recognition**
Every helpful action is acknowledged. Upvoting a tip, re-confirming accuracy, publishing a TripLog — each earns visible score, tier progress, or a streak extension. The platform should feel like it appreciates you.

## 9. Trust & Safety Framework

### 9.1 Defense-in-Depth Model

Bhromon's content moderation operates across six independent layers:

```
Layer 1: Pre-publish gates (client-side validation)
Layer 2: Trust tier gating (Unverified cannot post TripLogs)
Layer 3: Community flagging (4 flag types + instant safety action)
Layer 4: Freshness decay (automatic staleness marking)
Layer 5: Cloud Function auto-moderation (threshold-based)
Layer 6: Human review (founder spot-checks + Region Expert council)
```

No single layer is sufficient. Content that passes Layer 1 can still be flagged at Layer 3. Content that passes all automated layers is spot-checked at Layer 6.

### 9.2 Explorer Score Formula

```
Score = (helpfulVotes × 2)
      + (tripsPublished × freshnessDecayFactor)
      + peerEndorsements
      − (spamFlagsReceived × 5)
      − (safetyFlagsReceived × 10)
```

**Key design decisions:**
- Safety flags penalize 2× more than spam flags — reflects real-world severity
- Trip freshness decay factor: score contribution decreases as published content ages
- Formula is fully public — visible in app, documented in help center
- No hidden multipliers, no A/B-tested invisible boosts

### 9.3 Flag Processing

### 11.4 Data Flow Patterns

**Read (cache-first):**
```
BLoC request → Repository → Hive (fresh?) → YES: return
                                           → NO: Firestore → update Hive → return
                                                  → Network error + stale cache: return stale + flag
                                                  → Network error + no cache: Left(NetworkFailure)
```

**Write (optimistic outbox):**
```
User action → Hive (optimistic write) → UI update
           → Hive outbox enqueue
           → Online? → SyncManager.flush() → Firestore
                     → Conflict? → MergeConflictDialog
                     → Network error? → backoff + retry
           → Offline? → ConnectivityService → auto-flush on reconnect
```

## 12. Offline-First System Design

### 12.1 Freshness TTL Matrix

| Content Type | TTL | Rationale | Stale Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| LocationPin | 4 hours | User location changes frequently | Re-fetch silently |
| TripLog (read) | 24 hours | Stable long-form content | Return stale + stale chip |
| ProTip | 1 hour | Decay state must be current | Re-fetch or show aging badge |
| ExplorerProfile (others) | 30 minutes | Score changes infrequently | Background refresh |
| ExplorerProfile (own) | Real-time stream | User needs live score | Firestore listener |
| OfflineBundle | Never auto-expire | Manual delete only | Manual refresh button |
| Feed content | 15 minutes | Freshness is a selling point | Pull-to-refresh |

### 12.2 SyncManager State Machine

```
IDLE → FLUSHING → SUCCESS (all synced)
              → CONFLICT (conflict detected, merge required)
              → RETRY (network error, exponential backoff)
              → FAILED (max retries exceeded, user notified)
```

**Backoff schedule:**
- Retry 1: 5 seconds
- Retry 2: 15 seconds
- Retry 3: 60 seconds
- Retry 4: 5 minutes
- Retry 5: 15 minutes
- After Retry 5: markFailed, FCM notification to user

### 12.3 Conflict Resolution Strategy

| Content Type | Strategy | UI |
|---|---|---|
| ProTip (edit) | Last-Write-Wins | None — transparent to user |
| LocationPin (edit) | Last-Write-Wins | None |
| TripLog (edit) | Three-way merge required | MergeConflictDialog |
| TripLog (delete) | Last-Write-Wins | Confirmation only |
| Vote / Flag | Server authority | Optimistic rollback if fails |

**Three-way merge dialog panes:**
1. **Original** — common ancestor (grey background)
2. **Your version** — local outbox copy (mint border)
3. **Server version** — current Firestore doc (gold border)

User options: "Use mine" · "Use server" · "Edit merged" (free-text editor)

## 16. Competitive Landscape

### 16.1 Direct and Indirect Competitors

| Competitor | Nature | Strength | Critical Weakness | Bhromon's Edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook Travel Groups | Massive, unstructured community | 200M+ users, zero cost to join | No verification, unsearchable, 100% online-only | Structured + searchable + offline + trust-scored |
| Google Maps / Lists | Global POI database | Authoritative location data, ubiquitous | No trip narrative, no cost data, no safety context | Rich itineraries, cost breakdowns, safety notes, hidden gems |
| Tour Aggregator Apps (GoZayaan, Sheba.xyz travel) | Booking-first platforms | Payment infrastructure, partnerships | Content exists to drive bookings — inherent bias | Content-first; zero booking pressure; trust is the product |
| Travel Blogs / YouTube | Deep individual expertise | High production value, personality-driven | Siloed, static, impossible to search across creators | Many-to-many graph; community re-verification; decay alerts |
| TripAdvisor (global) | Established review platform | Brand recognition, large database | BD content is thin and outdated; review-gaming rampant | BD-specific depth; structured trust score vs star-rating gaming |
| Local newspaper travel sections | Editorial authority | Trusted brand, professional writing | Infrequent, no real-time updates, no offline access | Real-time community updates; offline-first |

### 16.2 Why Incumbents Cannot Easily Copy Bhromon

**1. The Offline-First Architecture is a Genuine Engineering Investment**
Building genuine offline-first sync with conflict resolution is not a feature toggle — it's an architectural decision made from day one. Retrofitting this onto an existing booking-first app requires rebuilding the entire data layer.

**2. Trust Score Requires a Clean Incentive Structure**
A booking platform cannot credibly implement a trust score because its core business model (commission-driven recommendations) directly conflicts with unbiased trust signals. Any trust score they build will be distrusted by users who know the platform profits from specific recommendations.

**3. The Founding Explorer Network is a Relationship Asset**
30 trusted local experts who joined because of genuine belief in the mission cannot be replicated by a competitor offering higher payment — the relationship and shared values are the moat, not the content alone.

**4. Community-Owned Content Graph Compounds**
Every TripLog, every Pro-Tip, every confirmation adds a node and edges to a graph that becomes harder to replicate the longer it exists. A competitor starting today faces the same cold-start problem Bhromon solved at launch.

### 16.3 Competitive Positioning Statement

> "Bhromon is not trying to be a better TripAdvisor or a Bangladeshi Yelp. We are building the trust infrastructure that booking platforms, government tourism boards, and individual travelers will all eventually rely on as the ground-truth layer for Bangladesh."

## 24. Social Impact

### 24.1 Knowledge Preservation

Bhromon's Founding Explorer program — particularly recruitment of Local Sage profiles like Farida — creates a structural mechanism for preserving indigenous and local knowledge that has historically existed only in oral tradition or private conversation. This has value beyond commercial travel utility:

- Documentation of trail conditions, seasonal patterns, and local ecology contributes to a long-term community knowledge archive
- CHT community members gain a platform for sharing their expertise on their own terms, with attribution and (where structured) compensation
- Knowledge that would otherwise be lost as older community members retire or pass away gains permanence

### 24.2 Economic Opportunity Distribution

- Premium Guide marketplace creates a new income stream for skilled local guides, photographers, and writers — particularly valuable in economically underserved regions like CHT and haor areas
- Founding Explorer stipends provide direct, transparent compensation tied to genuine contribution quality, not follower-count-based influencer payments
- Partner Pick program creates discovery channel for small, sustainable tourism businesses that cannot afford traditional advertising

### 24.3 Safety & Risk Reduction

- Real-time safety flagging and CHT advisory prompts directly reduce traveler risk in sensitive or hazardous areas
- Fair-fare Pro-Tips reduce exploitation of travelers by predatory pricing — particularly benefits lower-income domestic travelers (students, families) who are most vulnerable to overcharging
- Structured cost breakdowns improve financial transparency and planning for budget-conscious travelers

### 24.4 Environmental Consideration

- Future roadmap consideration: partner with conservation organizations to flag ecologically sensitive areas with visit guidelines
- Offline-first design reduces redundant data transfer/network load compared to constantly-reloading web-based alternatives
- Potential future feature: carrying-capacity alerts for over-visited fragile ecosystems (e.g., specific Sundarbans trails)
